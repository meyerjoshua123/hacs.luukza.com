Setup guide & reference

This guide covers version 0.3.0. If you're coming from an older version, your existing setup becomes the main room automatically. It keeps the same entities, and everything it has learned moves across.

Install

  1. Make sure HACS is installed.
  2. In HACS open ⋮ → Custom repositories, add https://github.com/meyerjoshua123/ha_hvac_simulators and choose Integration.
  3. Find HVAC Simulators in HACS, Download it, then restart Home Assistant.
  4. Go to Settings → Devices & services → Add integration → HVAC Simulators.

Updates: HACS shows new releases automatically. If an update hasn't appeared, open the integration in HACS and use ⋮ → Update information.

Rooms & zones

Configure → Add a room turns a Home Assistant area into a room with its own sensors:

  • Temperature: needed for the room's own detection and learning. A room without it (e.g. a bathroom with only a humidity sensor) still gets humidity rates and shower detection.
  • Humidity, CO2, air quality, door/window sensors: as for the main room.
  • Air volume: floor area × ceiling height, used for CO2, occupancy and fan airflow.
  • Known air gaps: small starts the room 1.5× leakier, large 2.5×, until the real rate is learned. Notes are for you, and show on the room's heat-loss sensor.
  • Room type: Bathroom turns on shower detection.

Room links (zones)

Configure → Room links: for each room, pick the rooms its air flows into. Linked rooms form a zone (links work both ways). Zones matter because:

  • An appliance in a room without a temperature sensor is observed by a sensored room in its zone, the main room first. Example: a bathroom fan clearing CO2 that the living-room sensor measures.
  • A shower in a zone stops the other rooms in that zone from blaming a humidifier for the rising humidity.

Each room gets its own device, placed in its area. It includes difference to main room sensors, handy for comparing the laundry with the living room.

Appliances

Add appliances during setup or under Configure → Add an appliance. Pick the area each one is in, so it's matched to the right room.

Extractor fan groups

One appliance can be a group of fans. Set the count, the names (e.g. Bathroom, Kitchen, Laundry), the airflow per fan (m³/h) and the power per fan.

  • How many are on comes from how fast CO2 clears beyond natural leakage. Until you calibrate, each fan is assumed to move its rated airflow.
  • Which ones: run a single fan and call hvac_simulators.calibrate with fans: [Bathroom]. Once every fan is calibrated this way, the Fans on sensor lists the likely ones.
  • Quick calibration: set the Fans running (calibrate) number to how many are on right now, including 0 with all of them off.

Fans are only credited when doors and windows are known to be shut. Without door/window sensors or a confident estimate, a fast CO2 drop is split between "fan" and "airing out".

Plugged-in appliances (heat sources)

Choose Heat source with a smart plug, then the kind: fridge/freezer, washing machine, clothes dryer, oven/microwave, stove/cooktop, or general (TV, iron, steamer, hair dryer, soldering iron…). Then pick the plug's power sensor.

  • Status sensor: active above the active threshold, idle between the two thresholds, off below the off threshold. Attributes include cycles, 6-hour duty cycle, the last active spell and the last run length (short pauses count as the same run). Defaults depend on the kind, e.g. a fridge is active above 30 W and a dryer above 50 W.
  • Heat effect: learned °C/h and %/h per kW, only while no HVAC is running and doors and windows are shut. When several run together, each one only gets credit for its own share. Dryers, stoves and washers start out assumed to add moisture.
  • The effect is subtracted before HVAC detection, so a running dryer isn't read as heating or a humidifier.

Use binary_sensor.<room>_appliance_heat_or_moisture as a condition in humidifier or dehumidifier automations, so they don't react to the dryer.

Want full washing-machine program detection? That's what WashData is for. HVAC Simulators deliberately keeps plug statuses basic.

Mode, state & cycling

Every simulated appliance has:

  • Mode: what it's set to (heat, cool, dry, ventilate, filter, on, off).
  • State: active (driving the temperature), idle (on but resting) or off.
  • Idle reason: holding setpoint, between compressor cycles (fan only), or setpoint satisfied, compressor off (fan only).

When the room falls, rises a little and falls again, the mode stays cool while the state cycles between active and idle. The turning points give the detected setpoint.

Cycling hidden by thermal mass

In heavy buildings the temperature can stay flat while the aircon cycles, because the building gives back the heat it removes. The compressor still shows in humidity as a regular swing every 15–25 minutes. The detector needs a clear periodic rhythm that is big enough, repeats, and is regularly spaced. It needs about 75 minutes of history before it can decide. While it's detected, the state follows the compressor, and the thermostat offset (room sensor versus the unit's own thermostat) is learned.

Manual mode, timeouts & switched off

  • select.<appliance>_manual_mode: auto (inferred), off, or one of its modes. While a manual heat/cool mode is set, the unit simulates thermostat cycling around the setpoint.
  • number.<appliance>_setpoint: the setpoint on the unit. Use detected setpoint clears it.
  • Manual timeout (per appliance): after this many minutes, manual mode and setpoint revert to auto.
  • Switched-off detection: if, in manual heat/cool, the room drifts the wrong way past the setpoint for the set time, with no compressor cycling seen, the unit is marked off and goes back to auto.

While a manual mode is active, it also teaches the model every 15 minutes (you can turn this off under Tuning).

Teaching & calibration

At first the physics rules make the calls. Every example you give is stored, and a nearest-neighbour model trained on your examples takes a growing share of each decision, up to 75% by default.

  • Suggestion is correct (button): confirms the current suggestion for that room.
  • Actual mode (teach) (select): pick what's really happening.
  • Needs feedback (binary sensor): on when an active suggestion is uncertain. That's the best moment to teach.
  • hvac_simulators.calibrate: record an appliance's mode, state, setpoint and filter status. Several calibrations are combined, newest weighted most.

Feedback also teaches each room's passive heat-exchange rate, and which appliance usually does what (e.g. heating is usually the heat pump, not the oil heater).

Power & energy

Each appliance gets a Power sensor. It uses the rated power for the mode when active; when idle, the inverter holding power or the fan-only power; for fan groups, power × fans on; otherwise standby. The Energy sensor (kWh, total_increasing) goes straight into the Energy dashboard.

PowerCalc: turn off Calculate energy for the appliance. Then point PowerCalc at the Power sensor, or use the On binary sensor with a fixed wattage.

Effectiveness & filter reminders

While a unit is actively heating or cooling, it measures how hard the unit pushes the temperature beyond passive drift. It compares that with a baseline built after the last filter clean, at the same indoor/outdoor gap. The result is rated Excellent → Great → Good → OK → Bad → Terrible. It needs about 20 active samples before a rating appears.

  • When the rating reaches your chosen level: a persistent notification, an optional push through any notify service, and the hvac_simulators_effectiveness_changed event.
  • After cleaning the filter, press Filter cleaned (or calibrate with filter: clean).

People from CO2

People are estimated from how much CO2 is being generated (dCO2/dt + air changes × (CO2 − outdoor CO2)), divided by what one person breathes out at their activity level. The activity defaults to sleeping during your sleep hours, and you can override it with the Activity level select.

  1. With everyone out and doors closed, set People home (calibrate) to 0. This teaches the room's natural air-change rate, which also sharpens fan detection.
  2. With a known number of people, enter the count (and activity with the service). This teaches one person's CO2 output in that room.

Troubleshooting

The suggested mode says "Collecting data"

Trends need a few minutes of readings (about a third of the trend window, at least 5 minutes). Check that the indoor temperature sensor is reporting.

It flips between causes on a quiet night

Sensors wander a little, and a 15-minute window can mistake that for real change. Teach it a few times, or raise the trend window to 30 minutes and the rate thresholds under Tuning. Regular compressor cycling is detected separately and isn't affected by these settings.

The aircon shows idle all night but I know it was on

If the room was already colder than the cooling setpoint, the compressor genuinely had nothing to do and only the fan ran. If it was cycling, the humidity pattern should appear after about 75 minutes. Setting the manual mode and setpoint, or teaching it, speeds things up.

It blames an extractor fan when I opened a window

Add door/window sensors to that room, or give it outdoor temperature and humidity so the open-window estimate has two signals to work with. Without either, fans and open windows are shown as equally likely.

The effectiveness rating never appears

It needs outdoor temperature and about 20 samples of active heating or cooling after warm-up. Short cycles take longer to collect.

A bathroom fan isn't detected

The fan's room needs to be linked (Room links) to a room with a CO2 sensor, and doors and windows need to be known closed.

Where is my data?

Learning is stored in Home Assistant's .storage folder as hvac_simulators.<entry id>. Nothing leaves your Home Assistant.

FAQ

Does it control my aircon?

No. It only observes and estimates. To control a unit, use its own integration, and use HVAC Simulators' sensors in your automations.

Does it work in °F?

Yes. Readings are converted to °C internally; entity units follow your settings.

Can I use it for several homes?

Yes. Add the integration once per home (space). Each has its own rooms, appliances and learning.
